# When a Boundary Claim Becomes Testable: A Criterion for Adjudicating Scale-Relative Claims, with One Coded Case

Dmitry Zharnikov

ORCID: 0009-0000-6893-9231

DOI: [10.5281/zenodo.22148374](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22148374)

Working Paper v1.0.0 – August 2026

---

## Abstract

Claims that a self-maintaining or cognitive system extends to some scale are chronically underdetermined: in the cases usually discussed the investigator draws the boundary, and can redraw it around whatever is interesting. The standing defence of realism about such boundaries concedes that boundary-drawing is interpretative and answers that interpretation is ubiquitous in science. That reply holds only while its unstated subject holds: the interpreter is the theorist. This paper specifies the domains in which that subject fails. A domain admits adjudication when its perimeter is fixed by a party indifferent to the hypothesis under test, is observable to someone who did not draw it, and moves only by dated recorded events. Within such a domain the measurable quantity inverts: not whether a boundary is drawn correctly, but what a hypothesis-independent perimeter fails to contain. Its principal yield is negative, excluding most domains in which the dispute is conducted, relative to hypotheses for which the investigator sets the perimeter. One acquisition, coded from two disjoint document sets, serves as an existence proof: an audited perimeter of 60 legal entities contains no upstream project, while .956 of the product's source composition originates outside it. The firm is an instrument, not a subject.

**Keywords**: system boundaries, Markov blankets, scientific realism, underdetermination, models, measurement, individuation, free energy principle.

---

A recurring class of claims in the philosophy of the life and cognitive sciences asserts that some self-maintaining or cognitive system extends to a particular scale: that a mind extends beyond the skull [@clark-1998-extended-mind], that an organism's boundary is marked by a statistical partition [@kirchhoff-2021-determine-boundaries-mind], that the relevant individual is a metabolic whole rather than a lineage-forming one [@dupre-2009-varieties-living-things]. The claims are not equivalent and are not all offered as metaphysics. What they share is a structural weakness. In the cases where they are usually pressed — a cell, a colony, an artificial agent, a person with a notebook — the investigator draws the boundary, and can redraw it around whatever turns out to be interesting. The evidence adduced for the claim is therefore partly constituted by the choice that the claim is about. This is a form of underdetermination that does not turn on unconceived alternatives; it turns on the partition being an input to the test rather than an output of it.

The most developed version of this complaint concerns the Markov blanket formalism. Bruineberg and colleagues distinguish the blanket as a formal property of a model from the blanket as a metaphysically loaded boundary of a real system, and argue that the second is reached from the first only by an interpretative step that the formalism does not license [@bruineberg-2022-emperors-new-markov]. The dilemma they press is that the formalism is either innocent and philosophically inert, or substantive and unearned; a realist reading of it needs an independent argument, and none has been supplied.

The live response is philosophical rather than empirical. Kirchhoff, Kiverstein and Robertson name what they call the literalist fallacy: "the fallacy of accepting or affirming instrumentalism based on the claim that the properties of active inference models based on the FEP do not literally map onto real-world target systems" (p. 639) [@kirchhoff-2025-literalist-fallacy-free-energy]. Their reply to the dilemma is that it is false, because a modeller may use an abstract formalism to represent a target system *indirectly*: accepting a link between Markov blankets and organism-world boundaries does not commit anyone to the claim that the formalism literally is the boundary. The formalism is "not literally true of target systems" (p. 653) but is an approximate way of representing their boundaries, and the conclusion drawn is deliberately modest — "a realist interpretation of FEP models is a live and tenable option" (p. 659).

**Their conclusion is about models, and this paper is about boundaries.** The distinction has to be drawn before anything else, because the two theses are separable and only the second is at issue here. Realism about active inference models as representations of target systems is one claim; realism about where any particular system ends is another. Kirchhoff and colleagues argue for the first, and in doing so rely on a claim about the second. Andrews has argued, and this paper grants, that treating the free energy principle as a falsifiable theory of systems is a category error, because the principle is a model structure to which construals are added [@andrews-2021-math-not-territory]. No test of the formalism is demanded below. What is at issue is a class of *construals* — claims that a given system extends to a given scale — and the instrumentalist reading of those construals is stated in its own voice elsewhere [@vanes-2021-living-models-life] rather than only through its critics. **Nothing in this paper shows that realism about active inference models is false.** It shows that one defence of realism about boundaries is unavailable in a class of domains, and empirically idle outside it.

Two features of that exchange motivate this paper. The first is that nothing in it identifies a measurement whose outcome a realist and an instrumentalist about *boundaries* would predict differently. Realism is secured by lowering what realism demands, not by winning an empirical contest, with the consequence that two parties who both accept the indirect-representation move have no observational disagreement left to have. The dispute is not merely unresolved; on the terms its principal exchange sets, it is empirically inert.

The second feature is the one this paper turns on. The defence concedes the investigator-drawn-boundary point outright, and the concession has a precondition that goes unstated. The passage is at p. 652:

> Bruineberg et al. [-@bruineberg-2022-emperors-new-markov] make a good deal out of the fact that the shift from Pearl blankets to Friston blankets requires an interpretative step. It requires interpreting Markov blanket states in terms of sensory and active states. However, any kind of interpretation is done by a theorist, rendering the identification of Markov blanket states arbitrary. The point about interpretation is fair. It is another thing entirely to infer that interpretation implies arbitrariness. Nevertheless, science is unlikely to progress in the absence of interpretation.

The wording is verbatim; only the embedded citation has been rendered in this paper's style rather than the original's, so that it resolves in the reference list below. The published text separates two things carefully. It concedes *that a theorist interprets*, and denies only that interpretation *implies arbitrariness*, on the ground that interpretation is what theorists always do and science does not proceed without it. That is a good answer, and it is available exactly as long as its subject holds: the interpreter is the theorist. Where a boundary is not drawn by a theorist at all — where it is drawn by counsel, registrars and auditors, for their own reasons, before any theorist arrives, and moved only by dated events they file — the premise does not obtain, and the answer is unavailable.

This paper takes that observation and asks what a domain must supply for the resulting space to be occupied. The answer is a criterion of three conditions, and the criterion is the contribution. Its principal yield is negative: it identifies most of the domains in which the boundary dispute is currently conducted as domains in which no observation could settle it, which is a stronger and more useful statement than the observation that none has. Where the conditions do hold, the measurable quantity inverts. It is not whether the boundary is drawn correctly but what a hypothesis-independent perimeter fails to contain, because content escaping such a perimeter is evidence, whereas content escaping an investigator-drawn one is an artefact of where it was drawn.

One acquisition is coded, from two document sets that share no document, to establish that the three conditions are jointly satisfiable and that the divergence they license is observable. The firm is an instrument here and not a subject. Nothing below attributes cognition, agency or mind to a firm, and no inference requires it; the argument would collapse into existing accounts of group agency and distributed cognition if it did. Nor is it claimed that acquisitions are a new natural experiment, or that coding legal against economic structure is a new method. Both are established, and the second is conceded in detail below.

## The criterion

### *Three conditions.*

A domain admits empirical adjudication of a boundary claim when three conditions hold together.

**C1, hypothesis-independence.** The perimeter is fixed by a party whose reasons are indifferent to the hypothesis under test — not indifferent simpliciter.

**C2, observability.** An audit or registry makes the perimeter inspectable by someone who did not draw it.

**C3, dated mobility.** The perimeter moves only by discrete dated events whose outcomes are recorded.

C1 does the work that answers the exchange above. Where it holds, the interpretative step at issue has already been performed, by someone else, for unrelated reasons, and the theorist's role is reduced to reading a line rather than choosing one. C2 is what prevents that reading from becoming a second interpretative step: a perimeter that only its author can see is a perimeter the investigator must reconstruct, and reconstruction restores the freedom the criterion is trying to remove. C3 supplies the temporal grain. A boundary that drifts continuously has no events at which a before and an after can be compared, so nothing about it can be observed to change; a boundary that moves by dated, filed, recorded acts does.

The three are jointly necessary and are not independently sufficient. A perimeter can be hypothesis-independent and invisible, observable and continuously redrawn, or dated and drawn precisely around the content at issue. Each of those failures is realised by an actual domain, and they are tabulated under *Scope* below.

### *Why indifference is narrowed, and what the narrowing costs.*

An earlier statement of C1 asked for a perimeter fixed by a party indifferent to the hypothesis, or by a party with no stake in any theory. That is not available, and the reason is instructive rather than incidental.

Iacobucci and Triantis give a capital-structure theory of legal partitioning in which assets are separated into distinct organizations to realise the gains from tailoring financial contracts to each partitioned asset group, and grouped in a single entity when those tailoring benefits are outweighed by the economies of unitary, firm-wide financing [@iacobucci-2007-economic-legal-boundaries]. On that account the perimeter is not fixed by an outside party at all. It is chosen by the firm, endogenously, and chosen *jointly* with the economic integration decision. A demand for an indifferent perimeter-setter would therefore exclude the domain this paper uses, and on inspection it excludes every domain, because no party is indifferent to everything.

The condition an instrument actually requires is narrower and is available: that the perimeter-setter be indifferent **to the hypothesis under test**. Counsel, registrars and auditors are intensely interested in tax, liability and financing. They are not interested in whether self-maintenance lives at the level of the operator or the system, and no capital-structure decision has ever been taken in order to settle that question. Exogeneity with respect to the hypothesis is what an instrument needs; exogeneity full stop is not on offer anywhere and never was.

This is a genuine weakening and is stated as one rather than smuggled. It has a price and it has a return. The price is that the criterion now fails wherever a perimeter *is* drawn with the content at issue in view, which is the first row of the scope table and the sharpest limit the criterion has. The return is that a condition previously asserted becomes mechanised. Iacobucci and Triantis build on the foundational legal fact that only legal persons may own property: a division of a corporation cannot own property, sue, be sued, or bind assets by contract [@hansmann-2000-essential-role-organizational]. Unsecured debt is a firm-wide obligation, common shares may be issued only firm-wide, governance rights are firm-wide under a single state of incorporation and a single board, and takeover defences cannot be adopted asset by asset. Their worked example of tracking stock is an attempt to obtain asset-specific financing *within* one entity, and a demonstration of why it fails.

Read from this paper's side, that is a demonstration that the perimeter cannot be finely redrawn by the party who would most like to redraw it. Moving it requires creating or dissolving a legal person, which is a filed, dated, audited act. C3 therefore follows from property and capital-structure law rather than from observed practice, and the argument for it is one this paper did not have to make for itself.

### *What the criterion excludes.*

The criterion's principal yield is negative. Applied to the domains in which boundary claims are ordinarily pressed, it excludes most of them, and it excludes them at C1.

**The exclusion is hypothesis-relative, and it divides into two classes that must not be run together.**

**Class A: partitions that exist only as modelling choices.** An evidentiary boundary derived from prediction-error minimisation, drawn so as to keep the mind skull-bound, is a partition the theorist supplies [@hohwy-2016-self-evidencing-brain]; so is the choice of which nested blanket to emphasise, which is fixed by explanatory interest. The notebook of the extended-mind literature is the same kind of object [@clark-1998-extended-mind]: what is at issue is whether to count it in, and the counting is the analyst's. These fail C1 outright, and they are where the principal disputants actually disagree.

**Class B: interfaces that exist independently, whose *status as the system boundary* is the modelling choice.** A plasma membrane is not an investigator's stipulation. It is a physical structure that exists whether or not anyone theorises about it, and immunological surveillance includes and excludes independently of any philosopher's hypothesis [@pradeu-2016-organisms-biological-individuals]. Metabolic wholes that do not coincide with lineage-forming individuals are a second such case [@dupre-2009-varieties-living-things]. Class B does not fail C1 as a class. It fails C1 *relative to hypotheses about which interface is the system boundary*, and it may well satisfy C1 relative to others — a physiological perimeter is fixed by processes indifferent to the extended-mind dispute, and is observable to parties who did not draw it.

The negative claim is therefore stronger than the observation that no discriminating test has yet been proposed, and weaker than a blanket exclusion of biological domains. It is that none could be proposed **for the hypotheses relative to which the investigator is the perimeter-setter**, because any observation about what falls outside such a boundary is an observation about a choice the observer made. Whether immunological or metabolic individuation can supply a hypothesis-independent perimeter for some boundary claim is an open question this paper does not close, and it is entered in the scope table as open rather than failed.

The defence quoted above is, on this reading, correct about its own cases and correct for a reason it does not state: in class A interpretation really is ubiquitous and really is the theorist's, so the concession costs nothing there.

The criterion's value, then, is not that it settles the dispute. It is that it partitions the dispute into a region where it cannot be settled by observation and a region where it can, and shows that the region usually discussed is the first one.

## The inversion

### *Boundary failure as the measurable quantity.*

Within a qualifying domain the question changes shape. The natural question — is the boundary drawn in the right place? — remains unanswerable, because "right" is defined by the theory under test and the perimeter was not drawn to serve it. The question that becomes answerable is what the perimeter fails to contain.

The asymmetry is the whole of the inversion. What escapes an investigator-drawn perimeter is an artefact of where the investigator drew it: move the line and the residual changes, so the residual carries information about the drawing rather than about the system. What escapes a hypothesis-independent perimeter is not under the investigator's control at all. The line was placed for reasons unconnected to the hypothesis, and the residual is whatever those reasons happened to leave out. A residual generated that way is evidence in the ordinary sense — it varies with the world rather than with the observer — and it is the quantity this paper proposes to measure.

**Stated in the vocabulary of measurement, the point is familiar and that is its strength.** A quantity cannot be measured with a standard that is itself in dispute in the hypothesis under test; measurement is model-based and depends on standards not under test in the measurement they support [@tal-2013-old-new-problems]. C1 to C3 are conditions under which a perimeter can serve as such a standard for individuation, and the inversion follows: what the standard makes measurable is the residual, not the correctness of the standard. A reader who says this is just the observation that an independent criterion of individuation lets you see what it fails to cover is agreeing with the paper, and the paper claims that ground rather than circling it.

This converts a concession into a measurement. That a perimeter does not contain everything the system inside it needs is not a discovery; stated abstractly it is a truism, and every account of organizational boundaries already accommodates it. What is not a truism is that the shortfall can be enumerated from documents authored by parties who were not asking the question, and compared against a substrate enumerated from documents authored by different parties who were also not asking it. The truism becomes a finding when the two enumerations are independent.

### *What would falsify it.*

**The inversion and the existence claim have different falsifiers, and they should not be confused.** The inversion is falsified if, in a domain satisfying C1 to C3, the residual of a hypothesis-independent perimeter turns out to be as classification-dependent as the residual of an investigator-drawn one — that is, if the asymmetry does not hold. Operationally this is tested by re-coding the residual under a classification rigged against the finding and observing whether the result survives; a residual that collapses under a defensible alternative coding was an artefact of the coding after all. The sensitivity pass reported below exists for this reason and is not decoration.

A second falsifier attaches to the existence claim rather than to the inversion: a case meeting C1 to C3 in which the audited perimeter does contain the components the system cannot persist without. That case is not merely conceivable. It is *predicted*, for a class of firms named in the scope table, and the prediction is what makes the coding a measurement rather than an illustration.

## The firm as an existence proof

### *Why a firm satisfies the three conditions.*

A firm with publicly traded securities satisfies all three conditions, and satisfies them for reasons that have nothing to do with any hypothesis about system boundaries.

C1 holds in the narrowed form. The perimeter of a corporate group is set by counsel and accepted by auditors, for reasons of tax, liability, financing and regulatory authorisation. Those reasons are neither disinterested nor arbitrary; they are simply unconnected to the question of where self-maintenance lives.

C2 holds because the perimeter is filed. A registrant's subsidiary schedule is an exhibit to an annual report, audited, dated to a fiscal year end, and available to anyone. The observability is not a courtesy: it is a claim that has been measured. Dyreng, Hoopes, Langetieg and Wilde benchmarked such schedules against what firms report privately to the revenue authority and found that "the overall incidence of nondisclosure is low, suggesting that most firms comply with Exhibit 21 disclosure rules, and that for most applications, Exhibit 21 disclosures provide a reasonable proxy for locations of significant subsidiaries" [@dyreng-2020-strategic-subsidiary-disclosure]. Nondisclosure, where it occurs, concentrates in tax havens, in firms under media scrutiny, and in firms carrying other markers of low-quality disclosure. The claim that an audit makes the perimeter observable is therefore not this paper's to assert. It has been measured by parties with access to the private benchmark, and the limits of the proxy are known.

C3 holds by the mechanism given above. The perimeter moves by incorporation, dissolution, acquisition and disposal — each a dated act with a filed record.

**C4 states what is coded.** The perimeter available is the audited *significant* perimeter, not an exhaustive enumeration of legal entities. Item 601(b)(21) of Regulation S-K permits omitting subsidiaries that, considered in the aggregate as a single subsidiary, would not be a significant subsidiary under Rule 1-02(w) of Regulation S-X. The exhibit coded below is headed as a list that *includes* the registrant's subsidiaries, and neither claims completeness nor invokes the omission provision. The paper's finding must therefore be one that incompleteness cannot reach, and the *Limitations* section shows that it is.

### *What is not being claimed about firms.*

The firm is an instrument for the boundary question, not a subject of it. No premise here attributes cognition, agency, mind or interest to a firm. The firm supplies a perimeter with three properties, and nothing in the inference requires it to be the kind of thing that has a perspective.

The disclaimer is load-bearing rather than decorative, because the nearest misreading is fatal. An argument that treated the firm as a cognitive system whose boundary is being located would reduce to existing accounts of group agency and distributed cognition, and would lose to them, since those accounts are more developed and were built for the purpose. The argument here is orthogonal: it uses a legally constituted perimeter as a fixed reference against which a substrate can be compared, in the way an externally calibrated ruler is used without claiming that the ruler measures itself.

## Related work

### *Four conceptions of organizational boundaries.*

Santos and Eisenhardt distinguish four conceptions of organizational boundaries — efficiency, power, competence and identity — and describe the efficiency conception as taking "a legal-ownership view of atomistic boundary decisions" [@santos-2005-organizational-boundaries-theories]. Their complaint about that literature is about dominance rather than merit: research is dominated by transaction-cost economics and related exchange-efficiency perspectives, and while useful, they argue, it is time to engage a broader view. The legal view appears among the efficiency conception's contributions, not among its limitations, and in summing up they call all four conceptions robust.

Their agenda asks specifically for research on the *relationships* among boundary conceptions rather than competition between them. That is what this paper does. Treating a legal perimeter as an instrument and what escapes it as evidence measures one boundary conception against another — the legal-ownership boundary against the competence boundary — and makes the relationship between them observable rather than argued. The paper answers a call; it does not defend a dismissed conception against a critique, and an earlier version of this argument that framed it that way was conceding a claim its source does not make.

### *Coding precedent, conceded.*

Coding legal structure from entity schedules, and coding the divergence between legal and economic structure from control rights, are established methods with decades of use, and the concession is unqualified.

La Porta, Lopez-de-Silanes and Shleifer trace control through chains of entities to identify ultimate ownership across countries [@laporta-1999-corporate-ownership-around]. Claessens, Djankov and Lang code the wedge between cash-flow rights and control rights in East Asian corporations [@claessens-2000-separation-ownership-control]. Almeida and Wolfenzon give a theory of why legal structure departs from economic structure by design, through pyramidal ownership [@almeida-2006-theory-pyramidal-ownership]. Altomonte, Ottaviano, Rungi and Sonno identify business-group boundaries algorithmically via the ultimate owner [@altomonte-2026-business-groups-knowledge]. Dyreng and colleagues, cited above for instrument validation, are themselves an instance: their subject is what entity schedules do and do not disclose.

What the precedent does not contain is the comparison made here. In every study above, the legal perimeter is coded against ownership, control, tax residence or geography. Each of those is itself a legal or financial construct, so both terms of every existing comparison are drawn from the same register, and the divergence measured is a divergence within one vocabulary. Coding the perimeter against *what the firm requires in order to keep producing* introduces a second term from outside that register. That is the unoccupied step, and it is a narrower novelty claim than any claim about the technique — which is precisely why it is defensible.

## Method

### *Case selection, and why the selection is itself an argument.*

Five acquisitions were checked directly against four filing criteria: a public acquirer, a public target, a dated close, and a filed merger agreement with post-close disclosure. All five qualify, so the choice is not forced by availability and requires justification on other grounds.

The case coded is IBM's acquisition of Red Hat, with a perimeter-event of 2019-07-09. The four others checked and not coded were Microsoft/LinkedIn (2016-12-08), Salesforce/Slack (2021-07-21), Microsoft/Activision Blizzard (2023-10-13) and Oracle/Cerner (2022-06-08). Three reasons select the case coded, and the third is the one that matters.

First, the escape is enumerable rather than rhetorical. Red Hat's persistence depends on upstream projects it does not own and could not have listed as assets, and this is visible in the perimeter document itself, by absence, rather than through an interpretive claim about tacit culture.

Second, IBM moved the legal perimeter while stating publicly that it would not move the operational one, which makes the decision not to integrate the operation a dated one rather than an inference from outcomes.

Third, and decisively, the case defeats the identification threat by construction. The threat is the one Iacobucci and Triantis supply with a mechanism: capital-structure demands are asset-contingent, and one contingency is opacity — assets that are difficult to value and monitor are more likely to be held in firms with private rather than public debt and concentrated rather than dispersed ownership. Tacit process and founder intent are paradigm cases of assets difficult to value. Opacity is therefore a candidate common cause of both where the perimeter is drawn and what escapes it, and a rival explanation with a named mechanism is not answered by acknowledgement.

This case inverts the confound. Its escaping content is maximally *transparent* — public repositories, public governance charters, public mailing lists, public release engineering. It is hard to own and easy to observe. A case in which the escaping content is highly observable cannot be explained by the claim that opacity drove the partitioning. The confound is designed out rather than conceded, which is what selecting on this ground buys.

### *The legal coding.*

**Rule: regulatory filings only.** Nothing on this side of the case is read from a community document, a repository, a blog or a press account.

The perimeter is enumerated from Exhibit 21.1 to Red Hat's Form 10-K for fiscal 2019, filed 2019-04-24 under accession 0001087423-19-000012, stating the subsidiaries as of 2019-02-28 — the audited fiscal year end, four months before the perimeter-event. The perimeter-event is decomposed from the Form 8-K filed 2019-07-09 under accession 0000950142-19-001516, covering completion, delisting, the disposition of the board and officers, and the treatment of the constitutional documents. A third filing, the definitive merger proxy on Schedule 14A filed 2018-12-12 under accession 0001193125-18-347874, supplies the retention and restrictive-covenant arrangements discussed under *Discussion*. All three are retrievable by accession number from the Commission's public archive.

**AA1, and its direction.** The coding assumes that a published subsidiary schedule is a faithful enough enumeration of the audited significant legal perimeter to support comparison. The assumption is relaxable and its relaxation is bounded: if the schedule under-reports, the coded perimeter is smaller than the real one, but an omitted entity is still a legal entity and cannot introduce an upstream project onto the list.

### *The persistence coding.*

The second coding was specified in full before any data was fetched, so its protocol is pre-registered rather than reconstructed. Two constraints bind it.

**Disjoint sources.** The persistence coding may read none of the filings used in the legal coding. If both sides are read from the same document, the divergence between them is an artefact of one drafter's choices and the argument is circular.

**No keying on valuation difficulty.** Difficulty of valuation is precisely the variable the identification-threat account says drives perimeter placement, so coding persistence content as "what is hard to value" would assume the conclusion. Persistence is coded on a criterion independent of valuation.

**The coding question**, asked of each candidate item blind to the entity list, is counterfactual necessity rather than value: would the firm's principal products still be producible six months from now if this item vanished, holding all owned assets and all personnel constant? An item is persistence content when the answer is no. The question is answerable from technical and governance documents without knowing what any legal entity owns, which is what makes the separation possible.

**Admissible sources** are public, non-regulatory, and authored for purposes unrelated to corporate structure. The data of record is the `primary.xml.gz` repository metadata for the BaseOS and AppStream *source* repositories of CentOS 8.0.1905 — the public rebuild of Red Hat Enterprise Linux 8.0, released 2019-05-07, which falls inside the window between the audited perimeter date (2019-02-28) and the perimeter-event (2019-07-09). This metadata is authored by packagers, for package managers.

**The unit** is one source package. **The proxy**: a package is coded inside the perimeter when its declared upstream host is Red-Hat-controlled or Red-Hat-sponsored, and outside otherwise. The host list was fixed before the data was read and is deliberately generous to the perimeter: it counts `redhat.com`, `fedoraproject.org`, `pagure.io` and `centos.org` as inside, although none of those is an entity on the subsidiary schedule — the Fedora Project is not a subsidiary of anything.

**AA2 and AA3.** Declared upstream URLs are packager-authored and can be stale or wrong; errors are bounded by the sensitivity pass and by excluding undeclared cases rather than pooling them. A public rebuild is assumed to preserve source composition apart from branding artefacts; this is checked directly rather than assumed, and the check is reported below.

**A pre-registered failure condition was fixed before the run**: an inside share above .500 would defeat the claim for this case. It is recorded here because a threshold set after the data is read is not a threshold.

## Results

No inferential statistics are reported. Both codings are complete enumerations of fixed, dated document sets rather than samples from a population, so the quantities below are counts and shares, and no confidence statement attaches to them. The magnitudes are reported directly, and the sensitivity pass rather than a significance test is what establishes that they do not depend on where a line was drawn.

### *The legal perimeter.*

The audited perimeter comprises **60 subsidiary legal entities across 39 jurisdictions**, stated as of 2019-02-28. The entities are of three visible kinds: national selling and service companies (Red Hat GmbH, Red Hat K.K., Red Hat Canada Limited and others); financing and holding vehicles (RP EMEA Holdings I and II, Red Hat SA I and II, Round Pond Unlimited Company, Varsity Gateway); and companies that arrived through earlier acquisitions and kept their own names (3Scale, Codenvy, CoreOS, eNovance, FeedHenry, Gluster, Qumranet, Y.G. Noobaa).

**The finding is what the list does not contain.** No entity on it is an upstream project. Not the Linux kernel, not Fedora, not GNOME, not systemd, not Kubernetes: none of the artefacts on which the firm's principal products are built appears as the property of any of the 60 entities, because they are not the kind of thing a subsidiary schedule can hold. The perimeter is complete, audited and correct on its own terms, and it omits the substrate.

This observation is available *before* any interpretation. It is a fact about a list drawn by counsel for a regulator, not a reading of a culture, and its falsifier is trivially stated: an upstream project appearing as a subsidiary entity on the schedule.

Four things moved on different schedules. The legal person **persisted** — Red Hat, Inc. survived the merger, and what changed was the acquirer of record. The constitutional document was **replaced outright** by that of Socrates Acquisition Corp., a shell incorporated to effect the transaction, so Red Hat's certificate of incorporation ceased to exist while the corporation it constituted continued to trade, employ and ship. The board was replaced **entirely**; the officers were retained **entirely**. And registration ended **ten days after** control changed, so the reporting boundary and the control boundary carry different dates.

A boundary whose parts move on four schedules is not a single line, and no theorist chose where to cut it. That is C2 and C3 doing work rather than being asserted.

*Notes*: shares are of the 2,060 codeable packages, coded from the CentOS 8.0.1905 source repositories. The floor rows reclassify all 328 packages hosted on `github.com` as inside the perimeter; this is deliberately indefensible, since most of the hosting organisations in that set are unaffiliated with Red Hat, and is reported only to show that the result does not depend on where the line is drawn.

Of the codeable source packages, **.956 originate outside Red-Hat-controlled hosts**. The pre-registered failure condition was an inside share above .500. It was not met, and it was not close.

Eight components on which the product is uncontroversially dependent code outside on the same run, every one of them: `kernel` (kernel.org), `glibc` (gnu.org), `gcc` (gcc.gnu.org), `systemd` (freedesktop.org), `python3` (python.org), `openssl` (openssl.org), `bash` (gnu.org) and `coreutils` (gnu.org).

### *Sensitivity.*

Two robustness checks were run, both of them capable of overturning the finding.

The first reclassifies all 328 packages hosted on `github.com` as inside the perimeter. This is not a defensible coding — it credits Red Hat with the work of hundreds of unaffiliated organisations — and it is run precisely because it is generous to the alternative. The outside share falls from .956 to **.797** and the direction of the finding is unchanged. This is the falsifier for the inversion, tested rather than asserted: the residual does not vary enough with the classification to be an artefact of it.

The second checks that the CentOS rebuild has not stripped Red-Hat-originated components, which would inflate the outside share. Named Red-Hat-originated packages were sought directly and are present — `anaconda`, `insights-client`, `subscription-manager`, `redhat-rpm-config`, `redhat-menus` and `redhat-support-lib-python` — and 21 packages still declare a `*redhat.com` host. Only branding artwork is substituted (`centos-logos` for `redhat-logos`). The bias runs toward the outside category, is real, and is far too small to move .956 materially.

### *The divergence.*

The two codings meet here. The audited perimeter contains 60 entities and no upstream project. The product's source composition originates .956 outside Red-Hat-controlled hosts, with a floor of .797 under a coding rigged against the finding.

**No document is shared between the two document sets, and neither set was authored with the other in view.** One is regulatory filings drafted by counsel for a securities regulator; the other is package metadata written by packagers for package managers. The divergence is therefore not an artefact of one drafter's choices — the circularity threat the design was built to answer.

The stylised fact this supports is general in form and modest in scope: **a perimeter can be complete, audited and correct on its own terms while failing to contain what the system it encloses requires in order to persist.** The inversion explains it directly. Competing readings are available — an unusual business model, or capital-structure tailoring along some dimension other than opacity — and they are taken up under *Discussion* rather than dismissed here; what any of them must additionally explain is why the divergence is visible from two independent registers at once.

## Scope

### *Where the criterion applies, and where the firm application does.*

The criterion is only as useful as its limits are explicit, and there are two separate questions. Table 3 reads the criterion against the domains in which the boundary dispute is actually conducted; Table 4 reads the firm application against the domains in which a legal perimeter might be used as the instrument. Both are read against C1, C2 and C3.

**Table 3: Where the criterion applies to the domains the dispute is usually conducted in.**

| Domain | At risk | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Investigator-drawn Markov blankets; interest-relative nested blankets; the evidentiary boundary | C1 | Fails | Class A. The partition exists only as a modelling choice, so what falls outside it is an artefact of the choice. This is where the principal disputants disagree |
| Person-plus-notebook | C1, C3 | Fails | Class A. Whether to count the notebook in is the analyst's decision, and no party outside the dispute records when it enters or leaves |
| Immunological individuation | C1 | Open | Class B. The interface exists independently and includes and excludes without reference to the boundary dispute. It fails C1 only relative to hypotheses about which interface *is* the system boundary, and may satisfy it relative to others |
| Symbiotic and metabolic wholes | C1, C2 | Open | Class B. Lineage and metabolism supply more than one candidate perimeter, each independent of the hypothesis the others serve; whether either is observable at the needed grain is unsettled |

*Notes*: C1 hypothesis-independence, C2 observability, C3 dated mobility. Fails means the criterion does not apply; Open means the question is unsettled and is not closed here. Rows 1-2 are class A and rows 3-4 class B in the sense of the preceding section.

**Table 4: Where the firm application fails, degrades, or returns a null.**

| Domain | At risk | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Perimeters drawn with the persistence content in view — acqui-hires, intellectual-property holding companies, knowledge carve-outs | C1 | Fails | The perimeter is drawn *around* the content the hypothesis is about, so the perimeter-setter is no longer indifferent to the hypothesis and the escape is definitional again |
| Private firms | C2, C3 | Fails | No subsidiary schedule, no periodic report, no delisting or deregistration record. The perimeter is real and nothing makes it observable to a party who did not draw it |
| Special-purpose and variable-interest entities | C1, C2 | Fails | The entity exists precisely to ring-fence a defined asset pool, so C1 fails as in the first row; and consolidation turns on control, a judgement, so the reporting perimeter is an opinion rather than a registry fact |
| Decentralized autonomous organizations | C1, C2, C3 | Fails | Either no legal person at all, or a wrapper drawn around the code itself, which is the content. Where there is no registry there is no audited perimeter |
| Reporting-entity judgement under a control-based consolidation standard | C2 | Degrades | Control is assessed as power plus exposure to variable returns plus the ability to use the one to affect the other. The audit certifies a judgement, not a fact, so observability is weaker than under a bright-line rule |
| Tax-motivated consolidations | C2 | Degrades | Nondisclosure is low overall but concentrated in tax havens, in firms under media scrutiny and in firms with other low-quality-disclosure markers, so observability degrades exactly where the perimeter is most tax-driven |
| Single-entity firms | C2 | Degrades | The subsidiary schedule is trivial or absent. The perimeter is observable but has no internal structure, so nothing can be read off its composition |
| State-owned enterprises, mutuals and cooperatives | C3 | Degrades | The perimeter moves by political or administrative act. The events are dated, but outcomes are recorded thinly and on no fixed schedule |
| Vertically integrated proprietary vendors | — | Null | All three conditions hold, and the perimeter is expected to *contain* the persistence content, so the measurement should return little or no divergence |

*Notes*: C1 hypothesis-independence, C2 observability, C3 dated mobility. Fails means the criterion does not apply; Degrades means it applies with a loss that must be stated; Null means it applies and is expected to find no divergence, which is a result rather than a failure. The tax-motivated row rests on the disclosure benchmark of Dyreng and colleagues cited above.

### *Two rows that carry the weight, and one verdict the table cannot express.*

**The open rows are open on purpose.** Class B is where a reader most likely to reject this paper actually works, and the honest verdict there is that the question is live. Nothing below turns on closing it.

**The first firm-application row is the criterion's sharpest limit.** Wherever a perimeter is drawn deliberately around the knowledge at issue, the instrument is destroyed by exactly the deliberateness that makes the case look interesting. The criterion works only where the perimeter-setter had some other reason, which is why C1 was narrowed rather than abandoned, and why the narrowing has a cost that shows up here rather than in a footnote.

**The last row is the falsification case.** A vertically integrated vendor that owns its persistence content satisfies all three conditions and should show no divergence. The prediction matters more than it may appear: it is what makes the coding above a measurement rather than an illustration, because a criterion that returned the same verdict for every firm would be measuring nothing. It is also the structural answer to the objection that the coded case is idiosyncratic. The criterion does not claim that all firms diverge; it claims that where the three conditions hold, whether a firm diverges is a question with an observable answer.

**A fourth verdict belongs in the limitations rather than the table.** A domain can satisfy C1, C2 and C3 in full and still defeat the *coding instrument*, because its escaping content has no enumerable artefact. Demand-side escapes are the clear case: where what a firm depends on is the continued participation of parties outside it, there is nothing to enumerate and nothing to compare the perimeter against. That is a limit on the method, not on the criterion, and conflating the two would let a reader mistake an instrument limit for a scope limit.

Platforms are deliberately absent from the table, and their absence is a correction rather than an oversight. A platform owner's legal perimeter is well defined and audited while the economically load-bearing complementor ecosystem sits almost entirely outside it. That is the criterion working, not breaking. Platforms threaten a thesis on which firms are where boundaries are drawn *correctly*; they do not threaten the inversion, under which a large well-observed residual is the finding rather than the problem.

## Discussion

### *What the criterion does not do.*

The first objection a specialist will press is not the organizational one. It is that the paper changes the subject: showing that one firm does not own the kernel it ships adjudicates nothing about realism concerning active inference models, locates no Markov blanket, and settles nothing about whether minds extend.

That objection is accepted in full, and the paper's claims are already scoped to survive it. **Nothing here shows that Kirchhoff and colleagues are wrong about models.** Their conclusion is that a realist interpretation of those models is a live and tenable option, and no argument below touches it. What is shown is narrower and is about a lemma rather than a conclusion: the defence of that position leans, at one point, on the ubiquity of theorist-performed interpretation, and that lean is unavailable in domains where the perimeter was fixed by someone else first. Where the three conditions fail, the boundary dispute is empirically idle; where they hold, it is not. Neither claim requires the firm to be a mind, and neither entails anything about group agency, distributed cognition, or the truth of the free energy principle.

The firm case earns its place by discharging exactly one burden — that the three conditions are jointly satisfiable by something real, rather than being a specification with no instances. That is all an existence proof is for.

### *The lead objection from organization theory.*

The second objection is that the paper restates something already known:

> This is the familiar fact that legal entity is not economic organization. Measuring it does not adjudicate anything unless you show an inference those literatures cannot already make.

The objection is partly right, and the honest status is that it is only partly ruled out.

What is familiar, and demonstrated repeatedly by the coding literature conceded above, is that legal and economic perimeters diverge. This paper adds nothing to that and does not try to. What is not available in that literature is a comparison whose second term is production necessity rather than another legal or financial construct. Every study cited above compares the legal perimeter with ownership, control, tax residence or geography; all four are constructs of the same register, and a divergence between two legal-financial descriptions of a firm licenses conclusions about legal-financial structure. It does not license the inference drawn here, which is about whether a boundary claim is testable at all.

The inference this paper offers, and which the rival literatures do not make, is the one the criterion supplies: that a defence of realism resting on the ubiquity of theorist-performed interpretation is unavailable in a domain where the interpretation was performed by someone else first. Neither "draw the correct boundary" nor "boundaries are blurred" yields that, because both still assume the drawing is ours to do. Whether that inference is worth the apparatus is a judgement a reader is entitled to make differently; what is not open is the claim that it is already made elsewhere.

### *Identification.*

The rival explanation with a mechanism is that the divergence is an artefact of the perimeter being drawn for capital-structure reasons that correlate with the opacity of the assets, so that what escapes is selected by the same variable that placed the perimeter.

This is rejected by design rather than by argument. The case was selected so that the escaping content is maximally transparent: public repositories, public governance, public release engineering. Opacity cannot explain a partition whose residual is fully observable. That is the reason this case was preferred to four others that met the same filing criteria, and it is why the selection rationale is stated in the method rather than left implicit.

Two further features of the identification-threat account help rather than hurt. Its predictions are largely untested and its authors say so, which places any coded case inside a live empirical claim rather than beside it. And it barely discusses knowledge at all: partitioning there runs on financing and governance, not on content. If perimeter placement is explained by capital-structure tailoring and persistence content is explained by something else, the two are separable in principle *within the threat's own theory*, which bounds the confound rather than dissolving it.

### *The acquirer's own admission.*

In the retention arrangements disclosed in the merger proxy, each of four retained executives entered a new restrictive covenant agreement with IBM, carrying non-competition and non-solicitation covenants for the longer of three years post-closing and two years post-termination, together with perpetual confidentiality covenants.

A covenant restricting what a person may do elsewhere is only necessary for content the acquisition did not convey. Buying the entity buys its assets, its contracts and its records; nothing a departing executive could carry out of those requires a separate contract to protect. The covenant is purchased precisely because some of what makes a firm work leaves in a person when the person leaves — and it was drafted by the acquirer's counsel, for the acquirer's reasons, with no view about Markov blankets. That is C1 operating on the content side rather than the perimeter side.

The strongest single datum is the shape of the largest award: a restricted-stock-unit grant with a grant-date fair value of \$15,000,000, vesting one-third at year three and two-thirds at year four, with no accelerated vesting. It is person-specific, four years long, and priced against a person's continued presence rather than against a role that could be refilled.

**This evidence is supporting and never critical, and the distinction is enforced rather than noted.** Retention payments alone would not carry the inference: ordinary agency theory explains them completely, they appear in essentially every large acquisition, and their magnitudes track seniority. Reading them as a price for unregistered content would be an arithmetic that already contains its own conclusion. What the covenants supply is an admission by the acquirer that entity purchase did not convey the content. An admission is good evidence. It is not a measurement of what escaped, and it is not written as one.

### *Replication, and why one case is offered.*

A replication case has been identified and deliberately not coded: Oracle's acquisition of Cerner (perimeter-event 2022-06-08), whose escaping content is of a different kind — dependence on externally maintained clinical standards and terminologies rather than on unowned upstream code — and which is transparent and enumerable from a public certification registry, so that the opacity confound stays designed out.

It is deferred rather than pending, and the reason is a feature of what this paper claims. The paper makes an **existence** claim: that the three conditions are jointly satisfiable and the divergence they license is observable. One instance discharges an existence claim. A second case would answer a question this paper does not ask, while adding pages and attack surface to a demonstration that is dual-sourced, pre-registered, sensitivity-tested and carrying a failure condition it could have met and did not.

The idiosyncrasy objection — that an open-source firm's divergence is an artefact of an unusual business model — is answered structurally instead, by the null row of the scope table. An existence proof is entitled to a favourable instance provided the selection rationale is stated, and the rationale here is stated in three parts under *Method*.

## Limitations

### *Origin is not necessity.*

The measured share describes where a source composition comes from. It does not describe what proportion of it is counterfactually required. ".956 of the source composition originates outside Red-Hat-controlled hosts" is measured; ".956 of the product is counterfactually necessary and unowned" is not measured and is not claimed anywhere above. The long tail of any distribution includes small components a firm could plausibly re-create in six months with personnel held constant, which is precisely what the coding question asks and precisely what a host-based proxy cannot resolve package by package.

**The necessity claim therefore rests on the named components and never on the aggregate.** Each of the kernel, the C library, the compiler collection and the init system is individually sufficient for the argument: none appears on the audited subsidiary schedule, none could be reconstituted within the stated horizon, and the product does not exist without them. The aggregate establishes only that this is the *shape* of the whole composition rather than a handful of convenient dependencies.

### *The coding is mechanical, not blind.*

The strong form of the design is an independent coder who does not know the hypothesis. No such coder exists here, and a coder who already knows the argument is not blind however careful.

What was implemented is a *mechanical* pre-registered extraction: a fixed procedure over fixed sources answering a fixed question, executed without reference to the legal coding. That controls for outcome-driven selection. It does not control for hypothesis-driven design of the procedure itself. The difference between "coded independently" and "coded mechanically from independent sources" is real, and only the second is true here.

A related residual is disclosed rather than repaired. The provenance proxy misclassifies Red-Hat-led projects hosted on neutral infrastructure: `NetworkManager` codes outside (gnome.org), `dnf` codes outside (github.com) and `389-ds-base` codes outside (port389.org), though Red Hat leads all three. The error runs toward the outside category. **The pre-registered host list was deliberately not widened after these cases were seen in the output.** Tuning a classifier on rows it got wrong is what pre-registration exists to prevent, and the generous floor of .797 bounds one of the three cases while leaving the other two disclosed rather than corrected.

### *What is not shown about models.*

The paper does not show that realism about active inference models is false, and does not argue against it. It shows that a particular defence of realism about system *boundaries* is unavailable where the three conditions hold, and empirically idle where they do not. A reader who takes the argument as a refutation of the free energy principle, or of model-realism about it, has taken more than is offered.

### *What one case establishes, and what the perimeter is.*

One case establishes satisfiability, not generality. Nothing above shows that the divergence is typical, and the scope table's null row states a class for which it is predicted to be absent.

The perimeter coded is the audited **significant** perimeter, not the exhaustive one. Regulatory rules permit omitting insignificant subsidiaries, the exhibit is headed as a list that *includes* the registrant's subsidiaries, and the disclosure benchmark cited above finds nondisclosure low but real.

**That gap cannot reach this finding, and the reason is about kind rather than quantity.** The result is about what is *absent* from the schedule — upstream projects. Under-disclosure of subsidiaries cannot put an upstream project onto a subsidiary schedule, because an upstream project is not a subsidiary in the first place: the firm holds no equity in the kernel it ships. The known incompleteness runs in a direction that cannot touch the claim, however large it turns out to be.

Finally, a limit on the instrument rather than on the argument. Package metadata is authored by packagers and its declared upstream URLs are occasionally stale or wrong. Packages declaring no upstream at all are excluded from every rate rather than pooled with either side, because an empty field is a missing datum and not a classification.

## Conclusion

Boundary claims about self-maintaining and cognitive systems are underdetermined in the domains where they are usually pressed, and the standing defence of realism about them does not repair this so much as reinterpret it: it concedes that a theorist performs the interpretative step, and answers that interpretation is ubiquitous. That answer holds only while its unstated subject holds.

This paper has specified what a domain must supply for the answer to be unavailable and the dispute to regain empirical content — a perimeter fixed by a party indifferent to the hypothesis under test, observable to someone who did not draw it, and moving only by dated events with recorded outcomes — and has shown that within such a domain the measurable quantity is boundary failure rather than boundary correctness. Most domains in which the dispute is conducted do not qualify, which is the criterion's main and deliberately negative yield.

**What changes, and what does not.** For the exchange this paper enters, the consequence is that the realism-versus-instrumentalism dispute, as currently conducted about boundary claims, is empirically inert — and that this is inevitable in some domains and avoidable in others, which is a question of domain selection rather than of philosophical stance. What does not change is the standing of realism about active inference models, which no argument here touches. For scale-relative individuation claims generally, the consequence is a question to ask before the evidence: who fixed the partition, and were their reasons indifferent to the hypothesis?

One acquisition demonstrates that the conditions are jointly satisfiable and the divergence observable: an audited perimeter of 60 entities across 39 jurisdictions containing no upstream project, against a product whose source composition originates .956 outside Red-Hat-controlled hosts, coded from document sets that share nothing. The firm is the instrument, not the subject. What remains open is whether any domain outside the legally constituted entity can supply the three conditions, and whether a necessity share can be measured directly rather than carried by named components — neither of which this paper settles, and both of which it is now possible to state precisely enough to pursue.
